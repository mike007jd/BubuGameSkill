# UI/UX

Answer the player's current question beside the relevant action: where to act, what changed, or what to choose. Give each screen one primary action. Use actual item previews, meaningful icons, short action labels, and visible selected/available states. Reveal detailed rules in contextual help or expandable sections. Let a successful action advance the introduction; teach by doing, not by reading.

Keep previewing and equipping distinct actions with distinct feedback, so browsing never commits. For rich systems, disclose detail where the player is looking (a tooltip on the slot, a panel on the item) instead of a rules screen. Judge themed or stylized controls at the ordinary camera and viewport, where they must still read as controls. Keep action costs, rewards, legal moves, and the active turn consistent with game state.

## Numbers

- Touch targets ≥ 44 × 44 CSS px; keyboard focus visible on every control.
- Text contrast ≥ 4.5:1; essential color signals paired with shape, label, or position.
- A first-time player completes the core action within 60 s of entry without reading a rules screen.
- Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`; offer a sound toggle.

Inspect entry, HUD, pause, results, and relevant inventory or shop through the normal journey. Preserve accessible names, focus return after modals, and comfortable touch targets.

## Done when

A first-time player can discover and complete the core action, understand its result, and continue. Record observed behavior and the actual source of feedback.
