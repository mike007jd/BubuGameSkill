# Game quality criteria

Use the axes relevant to this game's stage and fantasy. These are questions about the visible product and player behavior, not a mandatory scorecard. Pick the highest-impact gaps rather than filling every row on every task.

## Set the bar

For each chosen criterion record:

`intended result → observation conditions → measure or visible evidence → target/reference → observed gap → next change`

A useful target names the actual camera, viewport, ordinary route and state. Compare with a relevant reference at comparable framing and density. Set numerical targets from this interaction's timing, intended player, platform and accepted direction; project-specific scores, screen ratios and seconds are examples, not universal standards. When a judgment is aesthetic, state the comparison and concrete visual difference instead of disguising taste as precision.

Evidence has different meanings: source inspection establishes wiring; scripted routes establish sampled behavior; captures establish the visible sample; human play establishes that person's experience. Label the source. A script's success rate or a reviewer's score does not establish newcomer comprehension, enjoyment or retention.

## Visuals

| Criterion | What to observe or measure | Failure signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Composition and hierarchy | Subject screen size, framing/cropping, overlap, foreground/background separation, readable title/action hierarchy at ordinary viewports | Important characters are tiny or cut off; visual accents win attention over the hero or action |
| Character and object identity | Identity: silhouette, proportions and distinguishing features at playing distance. Stature: entrance, pose, setting, lighting and payoff together express the intended threat/role tier; inspect focal details where the camera exposes them | Different roles read as recolors; a larger final threat still feels less imposing than an ordinary one because its staging undermines the intended tier |
| Scene richness and spatial credibility | Authored focal/mid/background layers, believable scale and contact, purposeful detail, usable paths and variety in ordinary views | An attractive showcase hides empty routes; decoration floats, intersects or conflicts with reachable space |
| Material, light and contact | Material distinctions, surface response, motivated light, contact shadows and focal detail under normal game lighting; isolate layers to locate a defect | More glow conceals flat models; lighting turns important forms into silhouettes or blows away features |
| Style quality and coherence | Shared shape/material/color rules, controlled saturation and contrast, deliberate detail distribution across hero, environment and UI; compare to the accepted style reference | A style name is present but the result remains plain or unattractive; saturation or outlines flatten every layer |
| Camera and decision visibility | Continuous visibility of relevant actors, paths and targets; cue-to-decision window including reading and movement; clipping and axis stability during actions | Camera drama hides the usable response or enters geometry; a beautiful still gives too little time to act |
| VFX meaning and scale | Distinct anticipation, contact and aftermath; difference between routine and exceptional events; focal form readable at the effect peak and at high density | Every action is the same flash; a promised large action feels small; effects hide the actor, target or result |
| Motion and temporal integrity | Continuous clips of pose transitions, object contact, hit recovery, camera movement, looping materials and effects; compare cadence in normal and interrupted actions | Stills pass but motion flickers, snaps, floats, cuts abruptly or loses impact; hands/weapons miss their intended contact |
| World, UI and presentation unity | Identity, material cues and hierarchy across normal play, menus, equipment previews, loading and results; examine full state transitions | Game world and interface feel unrelated; menus look like a generic utility app; promotional views promise a quality ordinary play never reaches |

Inspect the conditions that expose the problem: sparse/dense, near/far, bright/dark, small/wide viewport, normal/peak effects. Choose relevant samples; a contact sheet makes comparisons easier, while continuous motion still needs a clip or actual play. Neither an isolated model render nor a cinematic showcase substitutes for the ordinary gameplay camera.

## Content and experience

| Criterion | What to observe or measure | Failure signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First complete loop | How a newcomer discovers the first action, understands its consequence and reaches the next meaningful action; time and help needed where observed | Rules exist but the player does not know what to do; an introduction ends before a playable payoff |
| Agency and effective choice | Reachable alternatives, when each is useful, their consequences and the foregone option; whether a choice changes behavior or only a label | A dominant always-correct option; apparently different choices have identical results; automatic play leaves no meaningful intervention |
| Behavioral variety | Distinct demands from encounters, routes, targets, enemies and modes; combinations encountered in normal sessions and the strategies they invite | New maps or items mostly rename/recolor the same demand; a larger catalog leaves the same repeated decisions |
| Progression payoff | Time to first meaningful upgrade, visible/behavioral change after it, later progression headroom and stalls; separate ordinary-earned and assisted starts | Unlocks arrive after interest has faded; upgrades barely change play; the second half saturates or a promised path is inaccessible |
| Pressure and recovery | Preparation time, difficult intervals, cognitive/action load and usable relief; low-resource duration or recovery time where relevant | Long unbroken stress, empty waiting, recovery that cannot actually be used, or difficulty that destroys the game's promised core pleasure |
| Effort and reward | Costs, time, risk, success distribution and reward meaning across plausible approaches; inspect ordinary/beginner/skilled paths only when evidence supports them | Reward inflation makes success arbitrary; early effort is unrewarded; a simulator persona is mistaken for real difficulty feedback |
| Purpose and consequence | Whether the player can explain the current objective, why an action matters, what changed and what follows; agreement between hints, rewards and actual state | More text without better understanding; decorative systems imply effects they do not have; narrative supplies names but no playable purpose |
| Reason to continue | What changes across the session or next attempt: mastery, new demands, strategy, discovery, story or visible progress; ask actual players when possible | Restart repeats the same experience; content is extensive on paper but voluntary replay or longer-session engagement remains unknown |

Use representative normal sessions to examine the applicable time scales: entry, first payoff, middle and late states. Genre-specific additions should make these observations easier, not require every game to add crafting, bosses, story, partners or an economy. Content quantities and level counts are inventory facts; quality comes from what those additions let the player do or understand.

## Game feel and sound

| Criterion | What to observe or measure | Failure signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Input acknowledgment | Input availability, acceptance, visible response and any dead interval; buffered/repeated/overlapping actions where applicable | A legal input feels ignored or waits for an unrelated animation; an input is accepted while the display still appears locked |
| Action–contact–consequence timing | Anticipation, authoritative judgment, visible contact, sound and outcome in sequence; event delays where measurable | Impact plays before contact or the result reads late; visual celebration contradicts the rules |
| Feedback hierarchy | Compare ordinary, successful, failed, recovered and exceptional actions through actor response, motion, sound and VFX | A reskin/rebuild removes the established intensity ladder; peak events lack a payoff or every event is equally loud |
| Flow through interruption | Repeated actions, cancellation, mode changes, pause/resume and recovery; how quickly the next intended action becomes possible | The game resets the rhythm, strands an input/state, or leaves residual effects at boundaries |
| Audio contribution | Actual listening: event sync, audible priorities, balance, variation, spatial meaning and repetition over a relevant stretch | Trigger logs pass but the mix is absent, fatiguing, misleading or dominated by repeated incidental sounds |

Choose timings from the action's purpose. Immediate acknowledgment and authoritative judgment can coexist with a deliberately delayed visual landing; synchronization means a coherent perceptual sequence, not forcing every event onto the same frame.

## Player journey and UI

| Criterion | What to observe or measure | Failure signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affordance and discovery | Whether ordinary input selects, starts and acts as expected; discoverability of targets and concise contextual help | Hover unexpectedly commits a choice; controls require knowledge of the implementation or an external explanation |
| Visibility and reachability | Critical labels/icons, legal destinations, target size, overlap, contrast and actual hit routes at relevant devices/views | Something looks actionable but cannot be hit; nameplates or models cover valid destinations |
| State honesty and readiness | Selected character/item, costs and availability; a visible transition until the new state/assets are ready | The previous character appears during launch; a spinner hides an avoidable state mismatch; completion/reward display disagrees with rules |
| Navigation continuity | Ordinary entry → play → pause/detail/shop → return → result → retry, limited to the game's actual screens | A nested panel changes pause/progress unexpectedly; focus, held input or the visible state fails to return |
| Teaching and information load | What explanation appears at a first event or repeated mistake; information at the active decision; alternate low-motion presentation | Opening instructions overload the player; hints are absent when needed, stale or unavailable to the actual interaction |

For requested runtime work, collect the smallest real route that exposes the criterion. Tests/builds run when the user asks. If runtime inspection or human feedback is absent, retain that boundary in the conclusion rather than replacing it with a score.
