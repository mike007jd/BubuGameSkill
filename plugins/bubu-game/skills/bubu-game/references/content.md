# Content development

Read the [content and experience criteria](quality.md#content-and-experience) to choose what needs improvement. Develop the game's intended fantasy and timescale; content quality is not a requirement to add every familiar game system.

## Diagnose the missing experience

Map what the player actually does through a representative session: first action, first meaningful payoff, later demand, recovery and continuation. Mark where a new decision or behavior appears, where the same strategy repeats and where the player only waits. Use the missing experience to choose the addition.

Count effective options by their consequences. Trace a choice from ordinary availability through its cost, effect, visible result and next use. Names, skins, menu tabs, level count and unused data fields are inventory, not evidence of strategic variety. Useful cosmetic content can still serve identity or collection without pretending to alter rules.

## Design a consequential addition

For a proposed option, encounter or objective, name:

- The player habit or decision it changes.
- The situation that makes the change useful and the alternative it competes with.
- Its readable preparation, action and consequence.
- How it returns to the existing loop and where it appears through ordinary play.

Differentiate new content through the demand it makes on timing, positioning, planning, attention or interpretation. Add quantity once representative differences are visible. Repetition may support comfort or mastery; judge its purpose rather than automatically replacing it with more complexity.

## Payoff, pacing and motivation

Place meaningful payoffs at points appropriate to the intended session. Observe first access, actual use, stalls and later saturation. Horizontal choices need understandable trade-offs; vertical growth needs a felt opportunity to use its strength. Reward numbers alone do not establish either.

Shape preparation, pressure and usable relief. Preserve the core pleasure while changing demands. Observe the actual hazards, information and resources left during a purported rest window. Account for reading and movement time before a decision closes.

Give the player an understandable reason for the current action and the next session or attempt. That reason can be mastery, strategy, collection, discovery, care, story or score. Add narrative only when it serves this game's experience; it can be expressed through state, actions, relationships and consequences rather than a larger text layer.

## Preserve the experience through revisions

When rebuilding or reskinning, list the core action sequence, intensity ladder and important payoff moments before changing them. Retaining rules while removing actor reactions, event timing, upgrade visibility or the ending can still flatten the game. Treat an intentional change to these as a design change, not an incidental art substitution.

Keep hints, action availability, costs and results aligned with the rules. A convenient fixture or direct interaction call can locate a problem but does not show that the player can discover, reach and execute it.

## Done when

The requested addition changes an observable part of the experience, appears through the intended route and returns a readable consequence to the loop. State what source/capture/play evidence establishes and which later-session or human judgments remain unknown. Use the user's quality bar; no fixed content count or mandatory playtest score applies to every game.
