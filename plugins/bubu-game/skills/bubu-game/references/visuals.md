# Visuals

Use the selected concept as the visual target and compare it with actual gameplay frames. Polish in this order: hero, camera, silhouettes, materials, contact with the ground, lighting. Spend detail where the normal camera reveals it. Keep hazards and interaction targets readable in motion and at arena edges: distinct silhouette, contrasting value against the ground, and a size that survives the ordinary camera distance.

Connect anticipation, response, and aftermath to authoritative game events. Tune animation, sound, particles, and camera accents around the action's consequence, using the feel numbers in [Gameplay](gameplay.md). Make the world respond to the player: tracks, contact marks, displaced material, and secondary motion that persists briefly and leaves a trace. Test interrupted, repeated, and overlapping effects.

Organize scene, water, materials, light, and grading into one coherent style with a written three-color palette plus accent. For Three.js, inspect the installed renderer and official addons. Keep intermediate lighting and color work in linear/HDR space; tone map and convert to display once. Check color and data texture spaces.

Combine contact shading, highlight glow, atmosphere, and grading according to the art direction. Use depth of field where gameplay-critical information stays sharp. Compare effects at matching cameras and states. Inspect ordinary and close views, resized viewports, and bright, dark, and busy scenes.

## Done when

Readable, coherent stills and continuous action at the agreed bar. Listen to play for timing, balance, repetition, and pause behavior; distinguish audio-trigger checks from listening evidence.
