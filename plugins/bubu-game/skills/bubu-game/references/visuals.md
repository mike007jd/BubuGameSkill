# Visual production

Choose the relevant [visual criteria](quality.md#visuals) and [feel/sound criteria](quality.md#game-feel-and-sound). Use the accepted reference as a comparison at the game's actual camera, viewport, density and action state. Style coherence and attractiveness are separate judgments.

## Compose at playing scale

Establish the hero, focal object, useful space and action hierarchy before adding detail. Compare screen size, cropping, overlap and foreground/background separation. Distinguish roles and threats through silhouette, proportion, pose and detail visible at the intended distance.

Spend richness where normal views expose it: focal forms, purposeful environment layers and believable spatial relationships. Inspect contact, scale and usable paths alongside the image. Preserve logical positions and collision unless changing those rules is authorized; crowded presentation can simplify occluded detail without moving the gameplay formation.

## Diagnose form, material and light

When an image fails, identify the layer responsible: shape/proportion, material response, lighting, atmosphere, grading or interface overlay. A neutral view or temporarily isolated layer can locate the cause; judge the delivered result under game lighting. Extra saturation, outline, glow and particles do not substitute for missing form or focal detail.

Carry shared shape, material, color and motion rules across world, characters, equipment previews, loading, results and UI. Adapt composition and detail to each view rather than pasting an identical image everywhere. Preserve distinctive identity and readable hierarchy.

For Three.js, inspect the installed renderer/addons and official APIs. Keep lighting/color work in linear/HDR space and apply tone mapping/display conversion consistently. Treat color and data textures according to their role.

## Judge action continuously

Inspect anticipation → action/contact → consequence → recovery at ordinary speed, with both isolated and repeated actions where relevant. Bind camera, animation, VFX and sound to actual game events. Distinguish routine feedback from success, failure, recovery and exceptional payoffs without obscuring the next action.

Keep relevant actors and response space visible through decision windows. Use stronger camera/effect accents where the interaction permits them; inspect clipping, axis changes and interrupted transitions. An attractive peak frame does not establish a readable attack or a stable loop.

Check actual contact and secondary motion: hands or weapons reach their objects, weight and ground contact read, and traces or aftermath respond coherently. Capture sets help compare states; clips expose flicker, snapping, drifting or cadence loss.

Listen to the game for synchronization, mix priorities, event variation and repetition. Audio-trigger checks establish event wiring, not listening quality.

## Done when

The relevant ordinary views and continuous action meet the agreed visual target, with coherent style and readable consequences. Record the states/viewports actually inspected and any unresolved motion, sound or subjective acceptance. Follow the [Workflow](workflow.md) for revisions involving several production lanes.
