# Gameplay

## Prototype

Choose the riskiest gameplay question: whether an action feels satisfying, a choice changes the outcome, or the camera makes interaction understandable. State the observation that would answer it. Build one short repeatable encounter with ordinary input, immediate feedback, and a way to try again. Keep the concept's defining camera, silhouette, and mood visible at a fidelity suited to this experiment.

Test a small set of meaningful choices, as in Storm-Race. Connect action, feedback, growth, and another attempt when that loop is the hypothesis, as in dustwake. Align input, contact animation, sound, and the authoritative outcome, as in Vibe Basketball. Exercise repeated and interrupted actions.

Run the Bubu Playtest Loop at prototype scope. Deliver a playable artifact, observed answer to the hypothesis, and the most useful next decision. A prototype request ends with that evidence; a full-game request continues from the validated interaction.

## Build

Expand the proven interaction into entry, core play, pause/resume, and replay or continued exploration. Give simulation state, input, rendering, and UI clear ownership. Use explicit time policies for simulation and presentation. Exercise focus changes, clear held inputs at mode boundaries, and release session resources on restart or scene replacement.

Show loading progress, actionable failures, and retry. Keep displayed success consistent with actual rules. For persistent progress or rewards, validate saved data and repeated settlement. Follow cavy-cottage's ownership lesson: opening nested panels and returning to play preserves the intended pause and progress state.

Bring in UI, visual, and performance references as those concerns become active. Complete the requested journey and content, then verify them through the Bubu Playtest Loop at the agreed quality bar.
